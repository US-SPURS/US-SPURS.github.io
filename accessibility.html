Accessibility Statement

The US Department of SPURS is committed to ensuring digital accessibility for people with disabilities. We are continually improving the user experience for everyone and applying the relevant accessibility standards.

Our Commitment

We are committed to providing a website that is accessible to the widest possible audience, regardless of technology or ability. We strive to comply with:

  • Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act
  • Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA
  • 21st Century Integrated Digital Experience Act (IDEA)

Accessibility Features

This website includes the following accessibility features:

Navigation and Structure

  • Skip navigation links for keyboard users
  • Consistent navigation throughout the site
  • Logical heading hierarchy (H1-H6)
  • Semantic HTML5 landmarks (header, nav, main, footer)
  • Breadcrumb navigation where appropriate

Screen Reader Support

  • ARIA labels and landmarks
  • Alternative text for images
  • Descriptive link text
  • Form labels properly associated with inputs
  • Screen reader-only text for context

Keyboard Navigation

  • Full keyboard accessibility
  • Visible focus indicators
  • Logical tab order
  • Keyboard shortcuts don't interfere with assistive technology

Visual Design

  • Sufficient color contrast (WCAG AA compliant)
  • Text resizing up to 200% without loss of functionality
  • No information conveyed by color alone
  • Responsive design for various screen sizes
  • Clear and readable fonts

Forms and Interactive Elements

  • Clear form labels and instructions
  • Error messages and validation
  • Required fields clearly marked
  • Time-outs with warnings (where applicable)

Conformance Status

WCAG 2.1 Level AA: Partially Conformant

"Partially conformant" means that some parts of the content do not fully conform to the accessibility standard. We are actively working to achieve full conformance.

Testing and Evaluation

We regularly test our website for accessibility using:

  • Automated accessibility testing tools (WAVE, axe)
  • Manual testing with keyboard navigation
  • Screen reader testing (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver)
  • User testing with people with disabilities
  • Code reviews for accessibility compliance

Compatible Browsers and Assistive Technology

This website is designed to work with the following assistive technologies:

Screen Readers

  • JAWS with Chrome, Firefox, Edge
  • NVDA with Chrome, Firefox
  • VoiceOver with Safari

Browsers

  • Chrome (latest version)
  • Firefox (latest version)
  • Safari (latest version)
  • Microsoft Edge (latest version)

Other browser and assistive technology combinations may work but have not been fully tested.

Known Limitations

Despite our best efforts, some limitations may exist:

  • Legacy PDF documents may not be fully accessible (we are working to remediate these)
  • Some third-party embedded content may not meet accessibility standards
  • Certain complex data visualizations may have limited accessibility

We are actively working to address these limitations.

Response Time

We aim to respond to accessibility feedback within 5 business days and will work with you to provide the information in an accessible format.

Alternative Formats

If you need information in an alternative format (large print, Braille, audio, etc.), please contact us using the information above.

Formal Complaint Process

If you are not satisfied with our response to your accessibility concern, you may file a formal complaint:

Internal Process

  1. Submit a written complaint to the Section 508 Coordinator (contact information above)
  2. Include your contact information and detailed description of the accessibility issue
  3. We will investigate and respond within 10 business days

External Agencies

You may also file a complaint with:

  • US Access Board: www.access-board.gov/contact/
  • Department of Justice, Civil Rights Division: www.ada.gov/filing_complaint.htm

Continuous Improvement

Accessibility is an ongoing effort. We are committed to:

  • Regular accessibility audits and testing
  • Training staff on accessibility best practices
  • Incorporating accessibility in all new development
  • Monitoring emerging accessibility standards and technologies
  • Engaging with the disability community for feedback
